## What makes it feel premium: ten observations

### 1. Photography supplies the color and the UI stays neutral
Chrome is white, off-white and near-black almost everywhere. Saturated color comes from photos: the Taichung rainbow village, Bali at dusk, a satay spread. The UI never competes with the image. This is the main reason it feels calm: the eye gets one source of color per screen.

### 2. One accent, used in very small doses
Bonvoy's orange/copper appears as:
- a 2pt line above the active tab bar item,
- the magnifier glyph inside the search pill,
- line icons on the stay hub (Call, Add to Calendar, Directions),
- the 2pt underline under the active segmented tab (Current / Past / Canceled),
- the single checkmark on "You're All Set".

It is never a button fill. **Primary buttons are near-black pills.** The accent tells you *where you are* and *what's alive*. It isn't used for "click me".

### 3. Emotional color is kept for identity moments
The one place color goes full-bleed is the **member/tier header** ([profile](https://mobbin.com/screens/bc72552d-a7be-493f-96da-dedfbfe45ed7), [level](https://mobbin.com/screens/d67a36e8-48a3-407a-8546-eea1b64a43ce)): a copper gradient, and a deep plum for Titanium. Color carries meaning about *you*. Promotions get photos, not color blocks.

### 4. Hierarchy comes from size and case, not weight
Large titles ("Trips", "Wishlists", "Review Reservation", "Security") are set in **regular weight** at ~28pt. Section labels are **small, uppercase, letter-spaced, grey eyebrows** ("RECOMMENDED FOR YOU", "SUMMARY OF CHARGES", "STAY INFORMATION", "CONFIRMATION NUMBER"). There is almost no bold. That restraint is what reads as "editorial": it's how a magazine masthead or a hotel folio is set.

### 5. Generous, consistent margins and slow vertical rhythm
There's a ~24pt side margin on every screen, 32–48pt between sections, and single-column flow. Horizontal carousels always **peek the next card** so you know it scrolls without needing pagination dots. Nothing is crammed. White space is used as a luxury material.

### 6. Few shapes, used consistently
- **Pill:** search field, primary/secondary buttons, filter chips, "Map" floating button.
- **Rounded rectangle (~14pt):** cards, with a very soft and wide shadow.
- **Circle:** quick-action buttons on the stay hub and tier benefits, avatars, date-range selections.
- **Sheet (~24pt top radius + grabber):** every secondary task, including room details, help, sign in, hotel messages and what's new.

Once you've seen a shape, you know what it does.

### 7. Text over images follows strict rules
Text on photos is always white, bottom-left aligned, on a soft dark scrim. Small metadata on images sits in **dark glass chips** ("Limited Time Offer", "[pin] Taichung City, Taiwan", "1/17", "Recently Viewed"). Brand logos sit in white directly on the photo. The rules don't vary, so a feed of very different photos still looks like one product.

### 8. Utility is plain and Apple-native
Underneath the editorial surface, the utility is very plain:
- the search pill has a two-line summary ("Japan / Jul 9–10 · 1 Room, 1 Guest") and a pencil to edit,
- a sticky bottom bar with "FROM 40 USD / Night" on the left and the CTA pill on the right,
- list rows built from line icon + label + chevron with hairline dividers,
- native toggles, native alerts, and Apple Maps,
- form fields that are **underline-only with uppercase labels**, like a hotel registration card.

Premium here means quiet competence. Nothing is custom for its own sake.

### 9. Empty states are designed, not left empty
Grey-scale illustration with **one** spot of accent (an orange roof, orange chair, orange blanket), a short title, one sentence, one black pill. The wishlist empty state uses a **dashed container**, a nice "placeholder for something you'll make" metaphor that Tripami should adopt.

### 10. The voice is warm and short, like a concierge
"Where can we take you?" · "We look forward to welcoming you." · "You're All Set" · "All set. Your reservation has been cancelled." · "Ready to add hotels to this wishlist?" Short sentences, second person, no exclamation marks.

## Weak spots, so we don't copy them

- **Low social presence.** The product is entirely about one person. There are no other people, no shared state and no activity, which Tripami needs at its core.
- **Information-dense legal and rate screens** (room rates, join consents) break the calm. Tripami should push that kind of detail into sheets or disclosure rows.
- **Uppercase in body content** (the guest form echoes user input in caps: "ALEX", "PLEASE COMPLETE") reads as shouting. Keep uppercase for system labels only.
- **Grey-on-grey sections** (sunken bands, faint borders) sometimes reduce contrast below AA for secondary text. Tripami's neutrals must pass contrast checks.
- **Generic hotel photography** in places (rooms, lobbies) makes cards feel interchangeable. Tripami's imagery is the group's own photos and the place, which is a strength we should design for.

## The principles we carry over

| Bonvoy does | Why it works | Tripami translation |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral chrome, color from photos | One color source per screen = calm | Neutral chrome; color from **places and people** (photos + member colors) |
| Accent only for state and location | Accent keeps meaning | Lagoon accent marks *you / now / selected*. It is never a big fill |
| Ink-black pill as the primary action | Confident, timeless, never fights photos | Same: **ink pill** is the primary action |
| Regular-weight large titles + uppercase eyebrows | Editorial hierarchy without shouting | Inter regular titles + eyebrows, plus a **serif (Newsreader)** for the diary voice |
| Saturated color only for identity | Emotion has a place | **Member colors** identify people; trip covers carry emotion |
| Sheets for every secondary task | Keeps context, feels native | Sheets for votes, invites, expense entry, item details |
| Date strip on the stay hub | Facts you need in one glance | **Trip strip**: Arrive · Leave · Nights · Travelers |
| Circular quick actions | Tool-like, tappable, calm | Circular actions: Invite · Add · Split · Map · Chat |
| Dashed empty containers | "A space you'll fill" | Same, framed as *the group fills it together* |
| Concierge voice | Warm, brief, trustworthy | **Friend-who-planned-it voice**: warm, brief, specific, with people's names |
